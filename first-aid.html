Psychological First Aid

If you or someone you are with is having a difficult time, start with what is needed now.

You do not need to explain everything, prove what happened or open a private case to find support here.

Immediate danger or urgent medical help

In Australia, call 000. For crisis support, 13 11 14 reaches Lifeline, available 24 hours, seven days a week.

https://www.lifeline.org.au/crisis-chat/ · Text 0477 13 11 14

Calls and messages open only when you choose a link. This page is not monitored.

If you feel overwhelmed or shaken

  1. If it is safe, move somewhere you feel more comfortable and away from immediate hazards.
  2. Consider one practical need: water, somewhere to sit, a quieter space or contacting someone you trust.
  3. Choose the help you want next. You can ask someone to stay with you or help you contact a service.

You can take your time. You do not have to tell the whole story.

Read the first aid sources

If you are worried about someone

  1. Look: check whether you and the person are safe and whether urgent medical help is needed.
  2. Listen: introduce yourself, ask what they need and listen without judgement. Give them time and avoid pressing for details.
  3. Link: offer practical help and connect them with someone they trust or an appropriate service, with their involvement wherever possible.

Respect the person’s culture, communication needs and choices. Ask before touching them. Give accurate information and avoid promises you cannot keep.

If you need care or ongoing support

You can ask for help before things become an emergency. There are options for children, young people, families, carers, people with disability, and people facing isolation, language or financial barriers.

Ask about fees, waiting times, local eligibility, interpreters and a support person. Medicare Mental Health Centres provide free support and normally need no referral; check local arrangements. They are not a crisis service.

If treatment or an order concerns you

You can seek independent help to understand your rights, raise treatment concerns or get advice about a hearing. An Official Visitor and a specialist legal service have different roles.

A complaint about care and an application to change a legal order follow different processes. Check the actual order and dates with a legal adviser promptly.

Read mental health rights and review routes

If you later want to record your account, the workspace is available. Keeping records is your choice and is not a condition of accessing support.

Sources and limits

This page offers basic practical support and service connections. It does not assess symptoms, diagnose a condition or replace professional care or first aid training.

The first steps draw on the World Health Organization guide to psychological first aid and the Australian Red Cross psychological first aid guide. ClarityPath has no claimed endorsement from these organisations.

For current local services, use the New South Wales Health support directory or Medicare Mental Health Centres. Emergency guidance: New South Wales Health. Service sources checked 10 November 2026; confirm current availability with the service.

ClarityPath does not send records or contact a service from this page. A service you choose to contact applies its own privacy and intake rules.
